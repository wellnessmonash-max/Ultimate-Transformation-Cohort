WHAT THIS IS

Think of your body as a phone battery.

Food is the charge. Movement is the system update. Sleep is the overnight recharge. Stress is too many apps running in the background. We do not need a “perfect” body; we learn how to take care of the one we have.

Important: This course is education, not medical treatment. If you are pregnant, under 18, have a medical condition, take medicines, or have pain/chest symptoms, speak to a qualified doctor before changing food or exercise.

YOUR 60-DAY ROADMAP

Six simple chapters, ten days each.

01 · Days 1–10

Know your starting point

Learn what “healthy” means for you. Notice habits without judging them.

What you will do + example

Try: Keep a three-day “real life” note: when you sleep, eat, move, and feel tired. Example: Riya notices she skips breakfast, gets very hungry at 4 pm, then eats chips. That is useful information—not a failure.

02 · Days 11–20

Build your plate

Learn protein, fibre, colour, water, and portions using normal Indian meals.

What you will do + example

The easy plate: half vegetables/fruit, one quarter protein, one quarter rice/roti/millet. Example: Dal + 2 rotis + cucumber-carrot salad + curd is a more balanced lunch than only rotis and pickle.

03 · Days 21–30

Move without fear

Understand strength, walking, stretching, and why sitting all day is hard on the body.

What you will do + example

Start tiny: walk 10 minutes after dinner, three days this week. Example: Arjun cannot do a push-up yet, so he begins with wall push-ups: hands on a wall, body straight, bend and push back.

04 · Days 31–40

Sleep and stress reset

Learn why tired brains crave quick food and why calm routines make habits easier.

What you will do + example

One change: park your phone away from the pillow 30 minutes before bed. Example: Instead of scrolling until 1 am, Meena showers, writes tomorrow’s top task, and sleeps at 11 pm.

05 · Days 41–50

Make habits stick

Use simple cues, planning, and kind recovery after an off day.

What you will do + example

Use “after I…”: “After I brush my teeth, I fill my water bottle.” Example: Missed a workout? The next action is a five-minute walk—not quitting the whole week.

06 · Days 51–60

Your personal forever plan

Choose the habits you can repeat when the cohort ends.

What you will do + example

Pick three: one food habit, one movement habit, one recovery habit. Example: “Vegetable at lunch, 20-minute walk on weekdays, lights out by 11:30 pm.”

THE BASICS, EXPLAINED

Big words made small.

Protein = the repair team

Your muscles, skin, and many body jobs use protein. Add a simple source at meals: dal, chana, rajma, curd, paneer, eggs, fish, chicken, soy, or sprouts.

Example: Replace a tea-and-biscuit breakfast with eggs and toast, or poha plus curd and peanuts.

Fibre = the broom

Fibre comes from vegetables, fruit, beans, oats, whole grains, seeds, and nuts. It helps food move through the gut and helps you stay full.

Example: Choose an orange instead of only juice; the whole fruit brings fibre.

Calories = energy units

Calories are not “bad.” Your body uses energy to breathe, study, walk, and play. The goal is nourishing food most of the time, not counting every bite.

Example: A birthday slice of cake is food, not a moral test. Enjoy it, then return to your usual next meal.

Strength = daily-life power

Strength training means safely challenging muscles. It can be chair-squats, carrying groceries, resistance bands, or weights.

Example: Sit down on a sturdy chair and stand up slowly five times. That is a beginner squat practice.

DAY 1 STARTER

Your first win takes 15 minutes.

  1. Drink a glass of water.
  2. Take a 10-minute easy walk, indoors or outdoors.
  3. Add one fruit or vegetable to a meal.
  4. Write one sentence: “I want more energy because…”

That is enough. Repeat before adding more.

COMMON QUESTIONS

No, you do not need to be perfect.

What if I miss a day?

Continue with the next lesson. One missed day is a comma, not the end of the sentence.

Must I give up rice, roti, or my favourite food?

No. Learn balance, portions, and what helps you feel good. Food traditions can fit inside a healthy routine.

Will this give everyone the same result?

No. Bodies, medical needs, starting points, and routines differ. We focus on safe habits and skills, not promises or comparisons.
